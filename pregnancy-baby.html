Pregnancy & baby memories

A pregnancy journal and baby memory book for the moments that matter.

There are changes you can see in a photograph, and feelings no camera can catch. A pregnancy journal and baby memory book can hold both — without becoming another thing to keep up with.

Start your journal on iPhone ↗

Private photo memories, in chapters called Eras.

A pregnancy journal for the in-between moments.

The big milestones deserve a place, but so do the ordinary Tuesday evenings: choosing a name, the first tiny kick, wondering what the future will look like. Give those details a line or two while they are still fresh.

  • Keep an ultrasound image beside the story of the appointment.
  • Write down a craving, a nickname or the music you played on repeat.
  • Capture how your expectations changed across the months.

A simple starting point

Try this in your own journal

01
Start before birth

Make a chapter for the months leading up to the arrival; add a first scan or a note to your future child.

02
Keep the firsts

Save a photo and a sentence for the journey home, a first smile, a first outing or a tiny new habit.

03
Leave room for real life

Some weeks are chaotic. A single image with the words “you finally slept” is enough.

Inside Stillkeep

See the little moments in one place.

A first morning home can sit beside the ordinary notes you make that week. One photograph and a sentence are often enough to bring the day back.

Build a baby memory book that sounds like your family.

A meaningful first-year record is not a checklist of achievements. It is a collection of details only you would notice: the way they curled their fingers, a favourite song, the person who could always make them laugh.

  • Choose a few recurring moments, such as monthly portraits or bedtime rituals.
  • Add the people, places and little phrases you want to remember.
  • Keep sensitive family memories in a private journal rather than a public feed.

Inside Stillkeep

Give the first year its own chapter.

An Era called “Baby’s First Year” gives those early moments a home. You can return to the first weeks without scrolling through thousands of unrelated photos.
