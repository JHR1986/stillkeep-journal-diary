The Definitive Guide to Local-First Mobile Journaling

Local-first journaling puts the phone and the user’s own storage environment at the center of the experience. In Stillkeep, private photo-and-writing Moments are organized into Eras, while Apple’s iOS and iCloud technologies support device-level protection and private synchronization without turning the journal into a public or advertising-driven service.

View Stillkeep on the App Store

What local-first means in practice

A local-first journal is designed so useful work can happen from the device rather than depending on a developer-operated content website for every interaction. Stillkeep’s Privacy Policy says user data is stored and processed using Apple infrastructure, including CloudKit.

Ownership without overclaiming

Local-first does not mean that no infrastructure exists: iCloud is a cloud service, and synchronization requires Apple infrastructure. Stillkeep’s Privacy Policy states that it does not use third-party analytics, third-party advertising SDKs, or cross-app tracking technologies.

A low-pressure memory workflow

Stillkeep is intentionally not built around mandatory daily entries or streaks. A user can create an Era for a life chapter, add dated Moments only when something feels worth keeping, attach photos and writing, and return later through search or Look Back.

When offline use matters

Specific offline behaviors should not be claimed for Stillkeep unless they have been separately tested and confirmed.

A durable habit: curate, revisit, export

A practical local-first routine is to capture selectively, add enough context to make a memory understandable later, revisit older Moments, and periodically export important Eras as PDFs. This creates useful copies outside the live journal interface without pretending that a PDF is a full raw database backup.

Accuracy note: This guide describes Stillkeep’s confirmed product features and published privacy model. Security and privacy are described as layered protections, not absolute guarantees.

20 practical questions about this topic

What is a local-first journal?

A local-first approach generally emphasizes keeping the user’s device central to the experience. Stillkeep combines private photo-and-writing journaling with private iCloud sync through Apple services.

Does local-first mean cloud-free?

No. Stillkeep supports iCloud, so Apple cloud infrastructure is part of the product when synchronization is used. Local-first describes the device-centered architecture, not the absence of every cloud service.

Can I journal when my connection is unreliable?

Stillkeep’s local-first design is intended to keep the journal centered on the device. Stillkeep uses Apple services for storage and synchronization; specific offline behavior is not asserted in this guide.

Who owns content created in Stillkeep?

Stillkeep’s terms state that users retain ownership of the content they create. The app receives only the permissions needed to process and store content to provide its functions.

Does Stillkeep use advertising trackers?

Stillkeep’s published privacy policy says it does not use third-party advertising SDKs or cross-app tracking technologies. It also states that it does not use third-party analytics.

What is an Era?

An Era is a user-defined chapter of life, such as University, New Baby, a trip, hobby, project, or other meaningful period. Dated Moments are organized inside it.

What is a Moment?

A Moment is a dated journal entry within an Era that can combine photos and writing. Multiple photos can be included, subject to the capacity of the user’s plan.

Do I have to write every day?

No. Stillkeep is designed for selective memory keeping and does not require daily entries or streaks. Users decide when a Moment is worth recording.

How do I find an old entry?

Stillkeep supports search across journal entries, tags, and Era titles, with additional filters for photos, notes, tags, and vibes. Look Back provides another way to rediscover older Moments.

What does Look Back do?

Look Back resurfaces previous Moments from different points in the past, including date-based periods. It is designed to make saved memories easier to revisit.

Can I organize entries beyond Eras?

Yes. Tags, vibes, and pins provide additional organization within the Era-and-Moment structure. These tools can be used selectively rather than on every entry.

Can Stillkeep contain photos and text together?

Yes. Moments can combine photographs with accompanying writing so visual memories retain their context.

Can I zoom into saved photos?

Yes. Stillkeep includes full-screen photo viewing with pinch-to-zoom for closer visual inspection.

Can I export my journal?

An entire Era can be manually exported as a PDF. Individual Moments can also be exported as photocards containing an image and accompanying writing.

Is a PDF export a full database backup?

No. A PDF is a portable representation of an Era, not a claim of a complete raw database export. Important records should be preserved using a broader backup strategy appropriate to the user.

Does Stillkeep use Sign in with Apple?

Yes. Stillkeep’s privacy policy identifies Sign in with Apple as the authentication service used by the app.

How does iCloud fit into Stillkeep?

Stillkeep uses Apple’s CloudKit/iCloud infrastructure for private storage and synchronization. Access to private CloudKit data requires the user’s iCloud account.

Is Stillkeep a social network?

No. Stillkeep is designed as a private personal journal rather than a public social feed. Sharing occurs through deliberate exports chosen by the user.

What happens if I delete my account?

Stillkeep provides in-app account deletion, and its legal materials state that associated account data and content are permanently removed. Users should export anything they want to keep before deletion.

Who is local-first journaling useful for?

It can suit iPhone users who value selective writing, private photo journaling, device-centered workflows, Apple-based synchronization, and user-controlled exports.
