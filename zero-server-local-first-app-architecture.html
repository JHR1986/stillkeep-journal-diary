Understanding Zero-Server and Local-First App Architecture

A careful guide to local-first and zero-server software concepts, with verified examples of how Stillkeep uses Apple infrastructure for private iOS journaling.

View Stillkeep on the App Store

What these architecture terms actually mean

Terms such as “local-first” and “zero-server” are useful only when they are explained precisely. A mobile app can emphasize the device while still relying on platform services for synchronization, authentication, purchases, or storage.

For Stillkeep, the most reliable description comes from its published product and legal information. Its Privacy Policy states that user data is stored and processed using Apple infrastructure and identifies CloudKit, Sign in with Apple, and StoreKit among the Apple services it uses.

Stillkeep's verified privacy model

Stillkeep is a private photo-and-writing journal organized around Eras and Moments. Its published Privacy Policy states that it does not use third-party analytics, third-party advertising SDKs, or cross-app tracking technologies.

Those are specific, verifiable statements and are more useful than claiming perfect privacy. Stillkeep's Privacy Policy appropriately notes that no method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

CloudKit and synchronization

CloudKit is part of Apple's infrastructure and is identified in Stillkeep's Privacy Policy. Stillkeep also supports private iCloud sync, allowing the product to use Apple's ecosystem for journal storage and synchronization rather than describing the architecture with unsupported absolutes.

This guide deliberately does not promise specific offline behavior or claim that data exists only on one physical device.

Data ownership and portability

Stillkeep's Terms state that users retain ownership of the content they create or store in the app. Stillkeep also provides deliberate export features: an entire Era can be exported as a PDF, while an individual Moment can be exported as a photocard with its image and accompanying writing.

Those formats provide practical portability, but they should not be represented as a complete raw database export.

Accuracy note: This page uses “zero-server” as a general software-architecture concept rather than as an absolute claim that Stillkeep uses no servers anywhere. Stillkeep-specific statements are limited to the product information and published legal/privacy information available for the app.

Questions about local-first and zero-server architecture

What does zero-server mean in software architecture?

Zero-server is an informal architectural description, not a guarantee that no servers exist anywhere. It usually refers to software that avoids operating its own conventional application backend for some or all core functions.

Is Stillkeep literally serverless?

Stillkeep should not be described as existing without servers because its published Privacy Policy states that user data is stored and processed using Apple infrastructure. The policy identifies CloudKit, Sign in with Apple, and StoreKit among the Apple services used by the app.

What is local-first software?

Local-first is a software design approach that generally places the user's device and locally available experience at the center of the product. The term can cover different implementations, so specific storage and synchronization claims should always be checked against the individual app.

How should Stillkeep's architecture be described accurately?

Stillkeep is a private iOS photo-and-writing journal whose Privacy Policy states that user data is stored and processed using Apple infrastructure. It uses Apple services including CloudKit, Sign in with Apple, and StoreKit.

Does Stillkeep operate its own journal-content server?

The public Stillkeep materials supplied for this guide do not establish enough detail to make a categorical claim about every backend component. The safest description is the published one: user data is stored and processed using Apple infrastructure.

Does Stillkeep use CloudKit?

Yes. Stillkeep's Privacy Policy identifies CloudKit as an Apple service used by the app for storing and synchronizing user data.

Does Stillkeep use iCloud sync?

Yes. Stillkeep supports private iCloud sync as part of its journaling experience.

Does using CloudKit mean data is stored only on the iPhone?

No such claim should be made. Stillkeep's Privacy Policy states that user data is stored and processed using Apple infrastructure, and CloudKit supports synchronization through Apple's services.

Does Stillkeep use Sign in with Apple?

Yes. Sign in with Apple is one of the Apple services identified in Stillkeep's Privacy Policy.

Why does Stillkeep use StoreKit?

Stillkeep's Privacy Policy identifies StoreKit among the Apple services it uses. StoreKit is Apple's framework for App Store commerce, including in-app purchases and subscriptions.

Does Stillkeep use third-party analytics?

Stillkeep's published Privacy Policy states that it does not use third-party analytics. This is distinct from making a broader claim that the app has no operational telemetry of any kind unless such a claim is separately documented.

Does Stillkeep contain third-party advertising SDKs?

Stillkeep's Privacy Policy states that it does not use third-party advertising SDKs. Stillkeep is offered through a free tier and a paid Premium subscription rather than an advertising-supported model.

Does Stillkeep use cross-app tracking?

Stillkeep's Privacy Policy states that it does not use cross-app tracking technologies. That is a specific published privacy commitment.

Does local-first automatically mean perfectly private?

No. Architectural labels do not create absolute privacy or security guarantees, and implementation details matter. Stillkeep's Privacy Policy also notes that no method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

Does iOS sandboxing protect app data?

Apple's iOS security model isolates third-party apps within application containers and restricts access according to platform permissions and entitlements. This platform protection should not be described as making any app immune from every security risk.

Does Stillkeep have an app-level biometric lock?

This guide does not claim an app-level biometric lock. Stillkeep relies on the protections provided by the iOS environment rather than advertising a separate biometric-lock feature.

Who owns content created in Stillkeep?

Stillkeep's Terms state that users retain ownership of the content they create or store in the app. Users are responsible for the content they choose to record and export.

Can Stillkeep users export their journal content?

Stillkeep can export an entire Era as a PDF and can export an individual Moment as a photocard containing an image and accompanying writing. These are user-controlled exports and should not be described as a complete raw database export.

How does Stillkeep organize journal data for users?

Stillkeep organizes life into Eras, with dated Moments inside each Era that can contain photos and writing. Tags, vibes, pins, search, and filters provide additional ways to organize and retrieve Moments.

What is the safest way to evaluate privacy architecture in a journal app?

Read the product's current Privacy Policy and Terms and distinguish documented behavior from architectural marketing terms. For Stillkeep, the verified public statements about Apple infrastructure, CloudKit, tracking practices, ownership, and security qualifications are more useful than absolute labels.
