Photo journal reference

What Should You Put in a Photo Journal?

By John-Henry Roche · Updated September 30, 2026

A photo becomes more valuable when the story around it survives too: who was there, where it happened, why the moment mattered and the small details a camera cannot explain.

This reference contains 100 photo journal ideas, each with a real example entry and a prompt to help you write it. It is inspiration, not a checklist. Keep what matters to you: a photograph, a few words of context, and enough detail for your future self or family to understand the memory.

How to use this page: browse for ideas and adapt them to your own life. You do not need to document everything. The strongest memory archives often preserve ordinary moments alongside major milestones.

The short answer

What to put in a photo journal

A good photo journal entry is one photo plus the details the photo can't show: who was there, where and when it happened, why it mattered, and one small detail you'd otherwise forget. A sentence or two is enough.

  1. The photoOne photo that captures the moment. Blurry is fine if it's the only one.
  2. WhoFull names. “She” means nothing to someone reading it in 30 years.
  3. WhereThe place, as specifically as you can: not just the town, but the café.
  4. WhenThe date, or at least the season and year.
  5. Why it matteredThe one sentence that explains why you kept this photo.
  6. One small detailThe joke, the smell, the song, the weather. This is what brings it back.

Photo journal ideas

Family & relationships

The people in your photos are usually the reason they matter. Write their names down, and what they were like at the time.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
New baby“First real laugh this morning; caught it in three photos.”What surprised you most about this week with them?
Children growing up“Lost her first tooth at the kitchen table and immediately asked about payment rates.”What is something they say or do right now that you'll miss when it's gone?
Parents“Dad fixing the gate again. He's been “nearly done” since March.”What does this photo show about who they are that a stranger would miss?
Grandparents“Nana's hands shelling peas on the back step, same as every summer.”What story were they telling while this photo was taken?
Partner“Asleep on the ferry after two hours of insisting he wasn't tired.”What did you love about them on this particular day?
Siblings“First time all four of us have been in the same room since Christmas.”What old joke or argument still comes up whenever you're together?
Extended family“Cousins' table at the christening; half of them I hadn't seen in ten years.”Who is in this photo, and how are they related to you? Write it down now.
Friendships“Twenty years of friendship and we still order the same thing.”How did you first meet, and what keeps you close?
Long-distance relationships“Screenshot of our video call: 7am her time, midnight mine.”What small ritual keeps you connected across the distance?
Family traditions“Christmas Eve pyjamas, year eleven. Nobody is allowed to opt out.”Who started this tradition, and how has it changed?

Photo journal ideas

Milestones & celebrations

Big days get photographed well but remembered badly. The details you're sure you'll never forget are usually the first to fade.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Birthdays“Forty candles, one attempt, and a very smug face.”What did you want from this year, and what did you actually get?
Anniversaries“Back at the restaurant where we had our first date. It's a phone shop now.”What has changed between you since the first one?
Wedding“Shoes by the window before everyone arrived.”What happened in the ten minutes before the ceremony?
Engagement“Ring box on the café table; she said yes before I'd finished the sentence.”Write down exactly what was said, while you still remember it.
Graduation“Mum cheered at the wrong name. We think she was warming up.”Who got you here, and what would you tell yourself on day one?
New job“Desk on day one: a plant, a lanyard and no idea where the kettle is.”What are you hoping this job will change?
Retirement“Dad's last day after 38 years. He brought the whole team scones.”What will they miss, and what are they most looking forward to?
Moving home“Every box we own, plus the kettle, which we packed last on purpose.”What are you leaving behind that doesn't fit in a box?
First home“Keys in hand on the front step. The door still sticks.”What did it take to get here?
Reunions“Class of 2006, back in the old hall. Somehow it's smaller.”Who had changed the most, and who hadn't changed at all?

Photo journal ideas

Travel & places

Travel photos pile up fastest and lose their stories quickest. One line per photo is enough to keep them.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
City trips“Morning light on a side street we found by accident.”What did you find here that wasn't in any guidebook?
Family holidays“Kids asleep in the back of the car before we'd left the county.”What will the children remember about this trip, and what will you?
Road trips“Petrol station breakfast, somewhere past Athlone. Best sausage roll of the trip.”What song was playing, and who picked it?
Weekend breaks“Two nights, no plans, one very good bookshop.”What did you need a break from?
Honeymoon“First morning as a married couple, and the coffee was terrible.”What do you want to remember about each other from this trip?
Camping“Tent up at the third attempt. The rain started at the fourth.”What went wrong, and why was it still worth it?
Hiking“Summit selfie, in fog so thick we could have been anywhere.”How did you feel at the top compared with the start?
Beach days“Sand in everything, including the sandwiches.”Who was there, and what was everyone doing just out of shot?
Favourite places“The bench by the harbour where I've eaten chips every summer since I was six.”Why do you keep coming back here?
Places lived“Our first flat's view: a car park and, if you leaned, the sea.”What was your life like while you lived here?

Photo journal ideas

Everyday life

Ordinary days are the ones you miss most later, and the ones you're least likely to write down.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Ordinary days“Coffee on the table while everyone was still asleep.”What does a normal day look like for you right now?
Morning routines“Same mug, same window, same robin on the fence.”What's the first thing you do every morning, and why?
Sunday afternoons“Roast in the oven, match on the radio, nobody in a hurry.”How does a good Sunday feel in your house?
School runs“Last school run before summer; she let me hold her hand to the gate.”What did you talk about on the way today?
Commutes“The 7:42 train: same carriage, same stranger doing the crossword.”What do you think about on the way to work?
Meals at home“Tuesday pasta, the recipe we've made two hundred times.”Who cooked, who complained, and what was said at the table?
Weather moments“First frost on the car. Scraped it with a loyalty card.”How did the weather change your plans today?
Small joys“The good biscuits, finally on offer.”What small thing made today better than it needed to be?
Quiet moments“Kids in bed, dishwasher on, one lamp lit.”What does quiet feel like in your life at the moment?
Life right now“Desk, laptop, three mugs. This is the year I'm building something.”If someone found this photo in 20 years, what would you want them to know about this time?

Photo journal ideas

Pets & animals

Pets change faster than we notice. Their habits, favourite spots and odd routines are worth writing down while they last.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
New pet“First night asleep under the chair instead of the new bed.”How did you choose them, and what was their first day like?
Puppy or kitten“Chewed one shoe from every pair. Consistent, at least.”What mischief are they up to this week?
Older pets“Grey muzzle now, but still meets me at the door.”What has changed about them, and what never will?
Walks with pets“Same route every morning; he still sniffs every single post.”Where do you walk, and who do you usually meet?
Pet milestones“First swim. Came out looking deeply betrayed.”What did they try for the first time?
Pets with family“The cat has chosen the baby. We are no longer relevant.”Who is their favourite person, and how can you tell?
Pet birthdays“Turned ten today; celebrated with a sausage and a long nap.”How old are they, and what are they like at this age?
Pet adventures“Escaped into the neighbour's garden and came back very pleased with himself.”What's the story behind this one?
Favourite pet places“The sunny patch by the back door, claimed every afternoon at three.”Where do they always end up, and why?
Pet companionship“Sat with me all through the bad week. Didn't ask a single question.”What have they helped you through?

Photo journal ideas

Home & making a life

Homes change slowly, then all at once. A before-and-after photo only means something if you remember the before.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Renovation“Wall stripped back before the new shelves went in.”What did this room look like before, and why did you change it?
Decorating“Three testers of nearly identical green. We chose the fourth.”What were you going for, and did it work?
Garden“First tomatoes. There were four, and we split them fairly.”What grew this year, and what didn't?
DIY projects“The shelf is level. The house is not.”What did you learn by doing this yourself?
Cooking“First attempt at Granny's stew: close, but missing something.”Where did this recipe come from?
Baking“Sourdough attempt six. It rose. We're not talking about one to five.”Who were you baking for?
Crafts“Half-finished scarf, now officially a two-winter project.”What are you making, and who is it for?
Collecting“The mug shelf is full. Buying another shelf.”Which piece in the collection has the best story?
Seasonal home“First fire of the autumn, and the dog has claimed the rug.”What does this season look and feel like at home?
Leaving a home“Empty kitchen on the last morning. The height marks on the door frame stay.”What happened in this home that you never want to forget?

Photo journal ideas

Personal history & legacy

Old photos are the most likely to lose their stories for good. Capture what the people who remember still know.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Childhood photos“Mum says this was taken outside our first flat.”What do you remember, and what have you only been told?
Old family photos“Grandad in 1962, in a suit he apparently borrowed for the day.”Who can still name everyone in this photo? Ask them this week.
Inherited photographs“Box of prints from Aunt Mary's attic. Nobody knows who the man with the bicycle is.”What do you know for certain, and what are you guessing?
Family recipes“Nana's handwriting on the brown bread recipe, butter stains included.”Who taught you this, and what was their secret?
Heirlooms“Great-grandmother's watch. Still ticks if you're patient.”Where did it come from, and who should have it next?
Letters & cards“Dad's postcard from his first job abroad, 1981.”What did it say, and why was it kept?
Then and now“Same steps, 30 years apart. Same haircut, sadly.”What's changed between the two photos, and what hasn't?
Life chapters“College years: the one photo that sums it all up.”What would you call this chapter of your life?
Stories behind photos“It looks like a normal picnic. It was the day Dad got the all-clear.”What is this photo really about?
Memories for children“Your first day at crèche. You didn't look back once. I did.”What do you want them to know about this day when they're older?

Photo journal ideas

Creative & reflective journaling

A little structure turns photo journaling into a habit. These ideas work well as regular rituals.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Photo-a-day“Tuesday: rain on the window and a very quiet house.”Why did you choose this photo out of everything you saw today?
Weekly photo review“Sunday: three photos that summed up the week.”What was the best moment of this week, and what was the hardest?
Gratitude photos“The neighbour who brought soup when we were all sick.”Who or what are you grateful for in this photo, and why today?
Seasonal journal“First daffodils on the roundabout. Spring is official.”What marks the start of this season for you?
Self-portrait journal“Mirror photo on my 35th. Tired, but I like her.”How do you feel about the person in this photo?
Creative projects“Page one of the novel, finally. It's terrible, and that's fine.”What are you making, and why now?
Reading life“On the train, with a bus ticket from 2019 as a bookmark.”What line or idea from this book stayed with you?
Personal reflections“The view from the walk I take when I need to think.”What were you working through when you took this?
Monthly recap“March in four photos: new job, old friends, one flat tyre, first sunny evening.”What will you remember this month for?
Year in review“Twelve photos, one per month. I didn't expect to be here a year ago.”What changed this year that you didn't see coming?

Photo journal ideas

Events & experiences

Tickets and videos don't tell the story. Who you went with and how it felt does.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Concerts“Lights came up during the final song; went with Nina.”Which song hit hardest, and who were you with?
Festivals“Wellies, mud to the knee and the best set of the weekend.”What was the moment of the weekend?
Sporting events“Last-minute winner. A stranger hugged me and I let him.”What was the score, and how did it feel in the ground?
Theatre“Programme and interval ice cream. Cried in the second half, as predicted.”What will you remember about the performance?
Museums“The painting I stood in front of for twenty minutes.”What stopped you in your tracks, and why?
Parties“The kitchen at 1am, where the best conversations always happen.”Who did you talk to, and what about?
Community events“Street party on our road; met neighbours we'd waved at for years.”What brought people together, and who did you meet?
Courses & workshops“Pottery class, week three. The bowl is a plate now.”What did you learn, and what surprised you?
Volunteering“Park clean-up: fourteen bags and one very old boot.”Why did you give your time to this?
Unexpected adventures“Missed the last bus, walked home at 2am, watched the sun come up.”How did an ordinary plan turn into this?

Photo journal ideas

Photo organisation & rediscovery

Some of your best photo journal material is already on your phone. These ideas rescue the stories hiding in photos you already have.

IdeaExample entryPrompt to write about
Camera roll favourites“Blurry photo, but the only one from that brilliant evening.”Why is this one a favourite, even if it isn't a good photo?
Screenshots worth keeping“The group chat the night we finally booked the trip.”What was happening when you took this screenshot?
Old digital photos“Found on an old laptop: our first holiday together, 2011.”What do you remember that the file name doesn't tell you?
Scanned prints“Scanned from Mum's album. Her note on the back says “Kerry, '84”.”What's written on the back, and what do you know beyond it?
Photos with missing stories“No idea where this was. Asked the family group chat.”Who might remember this? Ask them before you forget to.
Duplicate moments“Twelve near-identical shots of the cake; kept the one where everyone is laughing.”Which one captures the moment best, and why?
Favourite people“Every photo of Sam from the last five years, in one place.”What do these photos say together that none says alone?
Favourite places over time“The same beach, every August since 2015.”How has this place changed over the years, and how have you?
Searchable memories“Every photo from Dad's 70th, labelled so we can find them again.”What words will you search for to find this in ten years?
Photos to revisit“The day we brought him home. I look at it on bad days.”When should you come back to this photo?

For photographers

Photography journal ideas

If you take photos seriously, a photography journal helps you improve as well as remember. Record what you did, what worked and what you'd change.

IdeaWhat to record
Shot notesSettings, lens and light for your best frames, written while you still remember why they worked.
Light diaryThe same window or street at different times of day, with a note on how the light changed.
One-lens monthA month with a single focal length, and what it forced you to see differently.
Shoot reviewAfter every shoot, pick your three best frames and write one line on each.
The shot you missedDescribe the photo you didn't take. These entries teach more than the good ones.
Before and after editsSave the unedited and edited versions side by side, with notes on each decision.
Phone-only weekLeave the camera at home for a week and record what you notice instead.
Project seriesThirty frames on a single theme, such as doors, hands or your street, with a line per frame.
InfluencesA photograph that shaped your style, and what exactly you took from it.
Location scoutingPlaces worth returning to, with the best time of day, light direction and access notes.

Questions

Photo journal ideas: common questions

What are good photo journal ideas for beginners?

Start with photos you already have. Pick your five favourite photos from the last month and write one line about each: who, where and why it mattered. Ordinary days, camera roll favourites and a weekly photo review are the easiest ideas to begin with, because they need no planning.

What is a photo journal prompt?

A photo journal prompt is a question that helps you write about a photo, such as “What was happening just out of shot?” or “What do you want to remember about this day?” Prompts are useful when you have a photo you love but don't know what to say about it.

What can you put in a photo journal besides photos?

Short captions, longer reflections, dates, places and the names of people are the essentials. Many people also add screenshots, scanned tickets and letters, recipes, quotes, song titles and small details like the weather or what everyone was talking about.

How many photos should a photo journal entry have?

One photo is enough for most entries. Use a few photos when they tell one story together, such as a day out or a before-and-after. Choosing the one best photo is often what makes an entry memorable.

How often should you add to a photo journal?

As often as you'll keep it up. Many people add something weekly or whenever something worth remembering happens. A photo-a-day habit suits some people, but a weekly photo review is easier to sustain.

What is the difference between a photo journal and a scrapbook?

A scrapbook focuses on decoration and layout: photos, tickets and embellishments on a page. A photo journal focuses on the story: the words and context that explain why a photo matters. A photo journal can be simple, private and entirely digital.
